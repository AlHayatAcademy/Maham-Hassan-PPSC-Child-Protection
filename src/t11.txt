# Test 7 - Psychology and child development
The first psychological laboratory was established by Wilhelm Wundt in Leipzig in:|1879|1900|1859|1920|Wundt's lab in 1879 is conventionally taken as the birth of scientific psychology. William James wrote 'Principles of Psychology' in 1890.
Piaget's first stage of cognitive development is:|Sensorimotor stage (0-2 years)|Preoperational stage|Concrete operational stage|Formal operational stage|The four stages are sensorimotor (0–2), preoperational (2–7), concrete operational (7–11) and formal operational (12+). Understanding that objects continue to exist even when out of sight is called:|Object permanence|Conservation|Egocentrism|Centration|Object permanence develops towards the end of the sensorimotor stage (around 8–12 months it begins). Peek-a-boo games relate to it.

Abstract and hypothetical thinking develops in which Piagetian stage?|Formal operational|Sensorimotor|Preoperational|Concrete operational|Adolescents can think logically about abstract ideas and test hypotheses systematically.
In Piaget's theory, 'accommodation' means:|Changing existing schemas to fit new information|Fitting new information into existing schemas|Forgetting|Reflex action|Assimilation fits new experiences into existing schemas; accommodation modifies schemas. Equilibration balances both.
Erikson's psychosocial stage of infancy (0-1 year) is:|Trust vs. mistrust|Autonomy vs. shame|Initiative vs. guilt|Industry vs. inferiority|Reliable caregiving builds basic trust. Neglect and abuse in infancy can result in mistrust.
Which Erikson stage corresponds to toddlerhood (1-3 years)?|Autonomy vs. shame and doubt|Trust vs. mistrust|Identity vs. role confusion|Generativity vs. stagnation|Toilet training and 'no' stage. Harsh punishment can produce shame and doubt.
Erikson's 'Industry vs. inferiority' occurs at the age of:|6 to 12 years|0 to 1 year|3 to 6 years|12 to 18 years|School-age children learn competence; repeated failure or humiliation leads to inferiority.
Identity vs. role confusion is the main task of:|Adolescence|Infancy|Old age|Middle age|Teenagers explore who they are. Peer relationships and guidance are crucial. James Marcia extended the theory with identity statuses.
Erikson's last stage, ego integrity vs. despair, occurs in:|Old age|Adolescence|Early adulthood|Childhood|Older adults reflect on life. A sense of fulfilment gives integrity.
Freud's psychosexual stages in order are:|Oral, anal, phallic, latency, genital|Anal, oral, genital, phallic, latency|Latency, oral, anal, phallic, genital|Phallic, oral, anal, latency, genital|The stages are fixation-based: problems at a stage may cause adult traits. In Freud's structural model the 'reality principle' governs the:|Ego|Id|Superego|Libido|The id works on the pleasure principle, the ego on reality, the superego on morality (conscience and ideal self).
Which defence mechanism involves blaming others for one's own unacceptable feelings?|Projection|Repression|Sublimation|Regression|Projection attributes one's feelings to others. Repression pushes thoughts out of awareness; sublimation redirects impulses into socially acceptable activities; regression is reverting to earlier behaviour (e.g., a child wetting the bed after trauma).
A child who starts bedwetting and thumb-sucking after a younger sibling is born is showing:|Regression|Sublimation|Reaction formation|Denial|Regression is a return to an earlier developmental stage as a response to stress.
Kohlberg's theory is related to development of:|Moral reasoning|Language|Motor skills|Intelligence|Kohlberg proposed three levels (pre-conventional, conventional, post-conventional) with two stages each.
At the pre-conventional level of Kohlberg, morality is based on:|Punishment and reward|Social contract|Universal principles|Laws and order|Young children obey to avoid punishment or gain rewards. At conventional level they follow social norms and law and order.
The concept of 'Zone of Proximal Development' belongs to:|Lev Vygotsky|Jean Piaget|Sigmund Freud|B.F. Skinner|ZPD is the gap between what a child can do alone and with help. Scaffolding is the support given by a more knowledgeable person.
Attachment theory was developed by:|John Bowlby|B.F. Skinner|Albert Bandura|Ivan Pavlov|Bowlby proposed that infants have an innate need to form a close bond with a primary caregiver for survival. Early disruptions affect later relationships.
The 'Strange Situation' experiment was designed by:|Mary Ainsworth|Harry Harlow|Albert Bandura|Stanley Milgram|Ainsworth identified secure, insecure-avoidant and insecure-ambivalent attachment. Mary Main later added disorganized attachment, which is common among maltreated children.
Which attachment style is most commonly found in abused or maltreated children?|Disorganized|Secure|Avoidant only|Ambivalent only|Disorganized attachment shows contradictory, confused behaviour in front of the caregiver who is a source of both comfort and fear.
Harry Harlow's famous experiments with monkeys showed the importance of:|Contact comfort|Food|Light|Music|Infant monkeys preferred a soft cloth 'mother' to a wire mother that provided milk. It highlighted the need for affection.
Classical conditioning was discovered by:|Ivan Pavlov|B.F. Skinner|Albert Bandura|Carl Rogers|Pavlov's dogs salivated to a bell associated with food. Conditioned stimulus + unconditioned stimulus = conditioned response.
Operant conditioning is associated with:|B.F. Skinner|Pavlov|Freud|Piaget|Skinner showed behaviour is shaped by consequences, using reinforcement and punishment. Thorndike's law of effect preceded it.
Taking away a privilege to reduce a behaviour is an example of:|Negative punishment|Positive reinforcement|Negative reinforcement|Positive punishment|Negative punishment removes something pleasant. Positive punishment adds an unpleasant stimulus. Negative reinforcement removes an unpleasant stimulus to increase behaviour.
Giving praise to a child for finishing homework to increase future homework completion is:|Positive reinforcement|Negative reinforcement|Punishment|Extinction|Positive reinforcement adds a pleasant stimulus to strengthen behaviour.
Social learning (observational learning) theory was proposed by:|Albert Bandura|Pavlov|Skinner|Maslow|The Bobo doll experiment (1961) showed that children imitate aggressive models. Media and home violence can therefore influence aggression.
The 'Little Albert' experiment on conditioned fear was conducted by:|John B. Watson|Pavlov|Skinner|Bandura|Watson and Rosalie Rayner (1920) conditioned an infant to fear a white rat. It is considered unethical today.
The Binet-Simon intelligence scale was first published in:|1905|1950|1920|1999|Alfred Binet and Théodore Simon developed it to identify children needing special education in France.
The formula for IQ is:|(Mental Age / Chronological Age) × 100|(CA / MA) × 100|MA + CA|MA − CA|William Stern proposed the intelligence quotient. A child with MA 10 and CA 8 has IQ 125.
A child has a mental age of 12 and a chronological age of 10. IQ is:|120|100|83|110|IQ = 12/10 × 100 = 120. Scores 90–109 are average; 130+ are very superior; below 70 indicates intellectual disability.
Howard Gardner proposed:|Multiple intelligences|Triarchic theory|Two-factor theory|EQ|Gardner listed linguistic, logical-mathematical, spatial, musical, bodily-kinesthetic, interpersonal, intrapersonal and naturalist intelligences.
The 'g' factor of intelligence was proposed by:|Charles Spearman|Gardner|Sternberg|Goleman|Spearman's two-factor theory suggests general intelligence (g) and specific abilities (s).
Emotional Intelligence was popularized by:|Daniel Goleman|Piaget|Freud|Binet|Goleman's book (1995) highlights self-awareness, self-regulation, motivation, empathy and social skills.
Baumrind's parenting style that combines warmth and firm control is:|Authoritative|Authoritarian|Permissive|Neglectful|Authoritative parenting is associated with the best child outcomes. Authoritarian parenting uses strict control with little warmth.
A parent who uses harsh rules, punishment and no explanation is:|Authoritarian|Authoritative|Permissive|Democratic|Authoritarian parenting can lead to fearfulness, low self-esteem or aggression.
Neglectful (uninvolved) parenting was added by:|Maccoby and Martin|Piaget|Pavlov|Erikson|It is low in both responsiveness and demands, and linked with poor outcomes.
The Adverse Childhood Experiences (ACE) study was conducted by:|Felitti and Anda|Piaget and Erikson|Freud and Jung|Skinner and Watson|The 1990s Kaiser–CDC study linked childhood abuse, neglect and household dysfunction to later health and social problems.
Post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) symptoms in children include:|Flashbacks, nightmares, avoidance and hypervigilance|Increased appetite only|Improved sleep|Decreased fear|Younger children may show repetitive play of the trauma and regressive behaviours.

Play therapy is most appropriate for:|Young children who express feelings through play|Elderly people|Only adults|Only teenagers|Play is the child's natural language. It helps with trauma, grief and anxiety.
