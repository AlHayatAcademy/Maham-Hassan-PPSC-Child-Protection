# Test 5 - Social Work I
The father of social casework is considered to be:|Mary Richmond|Jane Addams|Octavia Hill|Saul Alinsky|Mary Richmond wrote 'Social Diagnosis' (1917) and 'What is Social Case Work?' (1922), giving casework a scientific basis. Jane Addams is associated with the settlement movement and Hull House.
Hull House, a famous settlement house, was established in Chicago in 1889 by:|Jane Addams|Mary Richmond|Samuel Barnett|Florence Hollis|Jane Addams and Ellen Gates Starr founded Hull House. She received the Nobel Peace Prize in 1931.
Toynbee Hall, the first settlement house, was founded in London in 1884 by:|Samuel Barnett|Jane Addams|Octavia Hill|Edward Denison|Canon Samuel Barnett and wife Henrietta founded it. The settlement movement placed educated volunteers in poor neighbourhoods to live and work with residents.
The Charity Organization Society (COS) was started in London in:|1869|1601|1834|1942|The COS emphasized investigation of applicants and coordination of charity. It is considered the forerunner of professional casework.
The English Poor Law was enacted in:|1601|1834|1869|1948|The Elizabethan Poor Law of 1601 recognized state responsibility for the poor, classified them into the impotent, able-bodied and dependent children. The 1834 amendment introduced the workhouse system.
Which social work method deals with an individual or family on a one-to-one basis?|Social casework|Group work|Community organization|Social action|Casework is a primary method that helps individuals solve personal and family problems through a professional relationship.
Which of the following is a primary method of social work?|Group work|Social action|Social welfare administration|Social work research|The primary (direct) methods are casework, group work and community organization. Secondary (auxiliary) methods are social action, social welfare administration and social work research.
The principles of casework relationship (seven principles) were formulated by:|Felix Biestek|Carl Rogers|Sigmund Freud|Abraham Maslow|Biestek's 'The Casework Relationship' (1957) lists individualization, purposeful expression of feelings, controlled emotional involvement, acceptance, non-judgmental attitude, client self-determination and confidentiality.
Which one is NOT among Biestek's seven principles?|Dependency|Acceptance|Confidentiality|Individualization|Dependency is not a principle; the aim of casework is to strengthen self-reliance. The seven principles are listed in the previous card.
'Client self-determination' in casework means:|The client's right to make his or her own choices and decisions|Worker decides for the client|Imposing solutions|Ignoring the client|Self-determination respects autonomy, though it has limits when choices harm self or others, or when the client is a child at risk.
'Controlled emotional involvement' means:|Worker responds sensitively but keeps professional objectivity|Worker avoids all feelings|Worker becomes emotionally attached|Worker punishes the client|The worker is aware of the client's feelings and responds appropriately without being overwhelmed. It differs from emotional detachment.
Which of the following is the capacity to understand another person's feelings by putting yourself in his or her place?|Empathy|Sympathy|Apathy|Antipathy|Empathy is feeling with the client while sympathy is feeling for the client. Empathy is a core skill for helping professions.
Perlman's problem-solving model identifies four Ps of casework:|Person, Problem, Place, Process|Policy, Plan, People, Profit|Power, Position, Person, Pay|Place, Plan, Program, Politics|Helen Harris Perlman (1957) later added Professional and Provisions. The model sees casework as a problem-solving process.
Which of the following steps of the casework process comes first?|Study (fact-finding)|Treatment|Evaluation|Termination|Classical process: study, diagnosis (assessment), treatment (intervention), evaluation, termination and follow-up.
The 'psychosocial' approach to casework was developed mainly by:|Florence Hollis|Reid and Epstein|Carl Jung|B.F. Skinner|Hollis's 'Casework: A Psychosocial Therapy' (1964) integrates psychological and social factors in understanding problems.
The task-centered casework model was developed by:|William Reid and Laura Epstein|Mary Richmond|Edward Thorndike|Erik Erikson|Task-centered practice is time-limited, goal-oriented and focuses on tasks the client can complete to resolve a problem.
Crisis intervention theory in social work was influenced by:|Erich Lindemann and Gerald Caplan|Pavlov|Piaget|Durkheim|Lindemann studied grief after the Cocoanut Grove fire (1942). Crisis intervention is short-term, focused and aims at restoring balance.
Group work as a social work method was pioneered by:|Grace Coyle|Mary Richmond|Edward Lindeman|Octavia Hill|Grace Coyle's 'Social Process in Organized Groups' (1930) was a landmark. Gertrude Wilson and Gisela Konopka are other major names.
The stages of group development by Tuckman are:|Forming, storming, norming, performing, adjourning|Beginning, middle, ending only|Study, diagnosis, treatment, closure|Plan, do, check, act|Tuckman proposed forming, storming, norming, performing (1965) and added adjourning (1977).
In which stage of group development do conflicts and struggle for power emerge?|Storming|Forming|Performing|Adjourning|During storming members challenge the leader and each other. Good group workers help manage conflict constructively.
Group cohesiveness refers to:|Attractiveness of the group to its members and sense of 'we-ness'|Size of the group|Age of the group|Fee of the group|Cohesion is the 'glue' that holds members together and affects participation and goal attainment.
Which of the following is the ideal size of a therapeutic group?|6 to 10 members|50 to 100 members|2 members|200 members|Small groups allow interaction and trust. Larger groups reduce participation and individual attention.
Sociometry, a technique to measure relationships in a group, was developed by:|Jacob L. Moreno|Pavlov|Freud|Weber|Moreno invented sociometry and psychodrama. A sociogram is a diagram of choices and relationships.
A diagram showing the family structure over generations is called a:|Genogram|Ecomap|Sociogram|Flowchart|A genogram records relationships, patterns and health history. An ecomap shows the family's connections with external systems.
Which tool shows relationships between a client and the social environment, such as school, work and agencies?|Ecomap|Genogram|Time-line|Sociomatrix|Ecomap (Ann Hartman, 1975) displays supportive and stressful connections between a family and outside systems.
Community organization as a method of social work was developed by:|Murray G. Ross|Mary Richmond|Freud|Maslow|Ross's 'Community Organization: Theory and Principles' (1955) described the 'consensus' approach with a focus on people's participation.
Rothman's three models of community intervention are:|Locality development, social planning and social action|Casework, group work and research|Primary, secondary and tertiary|Analysis, design and review|Jack Rothman (1968) described these models. Locality development emphasizes participation, social planning technical problem-solving and social action conflict and power.
Saul Alinsky is known for:|Conflict-oriented community organizing|Psychoanalysis|Casework theory|Systems theory|Alinsky's 'Rules for Radicals' (1971) promoted confrontation tactics to empower the powerless.
'Social action' as a method aims at:|Changing social policies, laws and institutions|Treating individuals only|Treating mental illness|Collecting funds|Social action involves advocacy, lobbying, campaigning and mobilizing communities for structural change.
POSDCORB, describing the functions of administration, was given by:|Luther Gulick|Henri Fayol|Max Weber|F.W. Taylor|POSDCORB stands for Planning, Organizing, Staffing, Directing, Co-ordinating, Reporting and Budgeting. It is widely used in welfare administration.
Systems theory in social work was influenced by:|Ludwig von Bertalanffy|Piaget|Pavlov|Skinner|General Systems Theory views individuals, families and organizations as interrelated parts of larger systems.
Bronfenbrenner's ecological systems model has which levels?|Microsystem, mesosystem, exosystem, macrosystem, chronosystem|Id, ego, superego|Oral, anal, phallic|Sensorimotor, preoperational|The ecological model explains child development as influenced by nested environments. It guides child protection assessment.
The 'strengths perspective' was popularized by:|Dennis Saleebey|Sigmund Freud|Emile Durkheim|Pavlov|It stresses clients' resilience, assets and capacities rather than pathology.
The 'person-in-environment' concept means:|Understanding the individual in the context of surrounding systems|Treating only the person|Treating only environment|Environmental science only|It is a signature social work perspective and underlines assessment of both personal and social factors.
Empowerment in social work means:|Helping people gain control over their lives and resources|Giving charity|Controlling clients|Isolating clients|Empowerment builds capacity, awareness and access to power. It is central to community development.
Rapport means:|A relationship of trust and mutual understanding|A court order|A payment|A report card|Establishing rapport is the foundation of any helping relationship and begins at the first contact.
In interviewing, an 'open-ended question' is one that:|Requires more than a yes/no answer|Can be answered by yes or no|Suggests the answer|Shows anger|Example: 'How are you feeling about your home situation?' Open questions encourage expression.
'Active listening' includes:|Paying full attention, paraphrasing and reflecting feelings|Interrupting often|Giving advice immediately|Judging|Active listening shows understanding and encourages the client to talk. It is basic in counselling.
The helping process in social work ends with:|Termination and follow-up|Intake|Assessment|Planning|Termination should be planned, discussed and followed up to ensure gains are maintained.
'Referral' in social work refers to:|Directing a client to another resource or agency|Closing a case|Giving medicine|Writing exam|A good referral involves explaining the purpose, making contact and follow-up.
A 'case record' is useful for:|Continuity of service, accountability and research|Entertainment|Gossip|Advertising|Records document history, plan, interventions and outcomes. They must be confidential.
Which of the following is a function of social work supervision according to Kadushin?|Administrative, educational and supportive|Legal only|Medical only|Financial only|Alfred Kadushin identified these three functions of supervision. Supervision improves quality and prevents burnout.
The international body of social workers, IFSW, was founded in:|1956|1900|1990|2010|The International Federation of Social Workers was established in 1956 (Munich). World Social Work Day is observed on the third Tuesday of March.
Which of the following is the professional value base of social work?|Dignity and worth of the person, social justice, service|Profit, power|Competition only|Privacy only|NASW and IFSW codes stress service, social justice, dignity, importance of human relationships, integrity and competence.
The Beveridge Report of 1942 identified five giant evils. Which is NOT one of them?|Crime|Want|Disease|Ignorance|The five giants were Want, Disease, Ignorance, Squalor and Idleness. The report laid the basis for the British welfare state.
Welfare state is a system in which:|The state takes primary responsibility for citizens' welfare|There is no government|Only private charity works|Only families work|It provides social security, health, education and housing. Examples include Scandinavian countries.
Social work with children in need of care requires primarily:|Knowledge of child development, law and family dynamics|Only legal knowledge|Only medical knowledge|Only accounting|Effective practice integrates skills across disciplines.
The term 'social diagnosis' was given by:|Mary Richmond|Piaget|Pavlov|Weber|It refers to the worker's understanding of the client's situation and personality in the social context.
Which method is used to bring about a change in laws like child marriage restrictions?|Social action|Casework|Group therapy|Case study|Social action mobilizes public opinion and lobbying for legislative reform.
Which is true about 'advocacy' in social work?|Speaking or acting on behalf of clients to secure rights and services|Ignoring client's rights|Punishing agencies|Private business|Advocacy may be case advocacy (individual) or cause advocacy (policy level).
