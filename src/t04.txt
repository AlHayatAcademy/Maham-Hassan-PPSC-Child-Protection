# Test 3 - UNCRC and international child rights
The UN Convention on the Rights of the Child (UNCRC) was adopted by the UN General Assembly on:|20 November 1989|10 December 1948|24 October 1945|20 November 1959|The Convention was adopted on 20 November 1989 and entered into force on 2 September 1990. The 1959 date belongs to the earlier non-binding Declaration of the Rights of the Child; 10 December 1948 is the UDHR.
The UNCRC contains how many articles?|54|30|41|60|The UNCRC has 54 articles: Articles 1–41 set out substantive rights, Articles 42–54 deal with implementation and monitoring.
Pakistan ratified the UNCRC in:|1990|1947|2001|2010|Pakistan ratified on 12 November 1990, among the early states. It withdrew its general reservation (subject to Islamic law and constitution) in 1997.
Which country has NOT ratified the UNCRC?|United States of America|India|Pakistan|Brazil|The USA has signed (1995) but not ratified the UNCRC. Almost every other UN member state is a party, making it the most widely ratified human rights treaty.
Article 1 of the UNCRC defines a child as a person below the age of:|18 years unless majority is attained earlier under national law|15 years|16 years|21 years|The Convention leaves room for earlier majority under national law, but the general standard is 18.
Which of the following is NOT one of the four general principles of the UNCRC?|Right to marry|Non-discrimination|Best interests of the child|Right to be heard|The four guiding principles: non-discrimination (Art. 2), best interests (Art. 3), life, survival and development (Art. 6), and respect for the views of the child (Art. 12).
Article 2 of the UNCRC deals with:|Non-discrimination|Best interests|Right to name and nationality|Child labour|Art. 2 requires States to respect rights of each child without discrimination of any kind, such as race, sex, religion, disability or status.
Article 3 of the UNCRC states that:|The best interests of the child shall be a primary consideration|Parents have no rights|States may ignore children|Children must work|Best interests is a primary consideration in all actions concerning children. General Comment No. 14 (2013) elaborates it as a right, principle and rule of procedure.
Right to life, survival and development is in Article:|6|12|19|28|Art. 6 recognizes the inherent right to life and requires States to ensure maximum survival and development, which includes physical, mental, spiritual and social growth.
The right of the child to express views freely and have them given due weight is in Article:|12|7|24|31|Art. 12 is the participation article. General Comment No. 12 (2009) explains the right to be heard in judicial and administrative proceedings.
Right to name and nationality is guaranteed by Article:|7|9|13|21|Art. 7 provides for registration immediately after birth, a name and nationality, and the right to know and be cared for by parents.
Protection from all forms of violence, abuse and neglect is covered under Article:|19|28|31|40|Art. 19 requires legislative, administrative, social and educational measures to protect children from violence in the care of parents or others. General Comment No. 13 (2011) explains it.
Article 28 of the UNCRC relates to:|Right to education|Right to health|Right to play|Right to privacy|Art. 28 recognizes the right to education, free and compulsory primary education. Art. 29 states the aims of education.
Right to rest, leisure, play and recreation is in Article:|31|24|32|40|Art. 31 includes cultural life and the arts. Play is not a luxury; it is essential for development.
Protection from economic exploitation and hazardous child labour is provided by Article:|32|34|35|37|Art. 32 recognizes the right to be protected from work likely to be hazardous or interfere with education, and requires a minimum age for employment.
Article 34 protects children from:|Sexual exploitation and sexual abuse|Child labour|Drug abuse|Torture|Art. 34 covers prostitution, pornography and inducement to unlawful sexual activity. Art. 33 protects from illicit drugs and Art. 35 deals with sale, trafficking and abduction.
Article 35 of the UNCRC deals with:|Abduction, sale and trafficking|Education|Health|Adoption|States must take all measures to prevent abduction, sale or trafficking of children for any purpose or form.
Article 37 prohibits:|Torture, cruel treatment, capital punishment and life imprisonment without release for under-18 offences|Education|Play|Immunization|Art. 37 also says detention must be last resort and children be separated from adults.
Article 38 of the UNCRC concerns:|Children in armed conflict|Health|Marriage|Adoption|It sets the minimum age 15 for direct participation in hostilities, later strengthened to 18 under the Optional Protocol on Children in Armed Conflict.
Article 40 of the UNCRC deals with:|Juvenile justice|Child labour|Education|Health care|Art. 40 sets standards on presumption of innocence, legal aid, speedy trial and privacy for children accused of offences, and encourages a minimum age of criminal responsibility.
Recovery and social reintegration of child victims is guaranteed by Article:|39|19|27|30|Art. 39 requires measures to promote physical and psychological recovery and reintegration of child victims of neglect, exploitation, abuse, torture or armed conflict.
Article 20 of the UNCRC is about:|Children deprived of family environment|Disabled children|Refugee children|Minority children|It entitles such children to special protection and alternative care, such as foster placement, kafalah of Islamic law, adoption, or suitable institutions.
Article 22 relates to:|Refugee children|Adopted children|Working children|Disabled children|Art. 22 requires protection and assistance for refugee and asylum-seeking children, including family tracing.
Article 23 recognizes rights of:|Children with disabilities|Orphans|Street children|Gifted children|Art. 23 says a mentally or physically disabled child should enjoy a full and decent life in conditions that ensure dignity and promote self-reliance.
Which UNCRC article deals with the child's right to health?|Article 24|Article 16|Article 8|Article 30|Art. 24 includes access to health services, reducing infant mortality, pre- and post-natal care, immunization, nutrition and abolishing harmful traditional practices.
Article 27 recognizes the right of every child to:|An adequate standard of living|Vote|Hold property only|Marry|Art. 27 makes parents primarily responsible but obliges the State to assist, particularly with nutrition, clothing and housing.
Article 16 of the UNCRC protects the child's right to:|Privacy|Play|Education|Health|It protects against arbitrary or unlawful interference with privacy, family, home or correspondence, and attacks on honour and reputation.
Article 8 concerns:|Preservation of identity|Right to marry|Abortion|Adoption|The State must respect the right of the child to preserve identity including nationality, name and family relations.
Article 9 states that a child should not be separated from parents unless:|Competent authorities decide separation is necessary in the child's best interests|The parents are poor|The neighbours object|The child is disabled|Examples include abuse or neglect. Even then the child has the right to maintain contact with both parents unless contrary to his or her interests.
The treaty body that monitors the implementation of the UNCRC is:|Committee on the Rights of the Child|UN Security Council|ICJ|World Bank|The Committee comprises 18 independent experts. States submit periodic reports (first within 2 years, later every 5 years) and the Committee issues Concluding Observations.
The Committee on the Rights of the Child meets in:|Geneva|New York|Vienna|Islamabad|The Committee is serviced by OHCHR in Geneva. UNICEF headquarters is in New York.
How many Optional Protocols does the UNCRC have?|Three|One|Two|Five|OP on the Sale of Children, Child Prostitution and Child Pornography (OPSC, 2000); OP on Children in Armed Conflict (OPAC, 2000); OP on a Communications Procedure (OPIC, 2011).
The Optional Protocol on Involvement of Children in Armed Conflict sets the minimum age for compulsory recruitment at:|18 years|15 years|16 years|14 years|States must ensure persons under 18 do not take direct part in hostilities and are not compulsorily recruited. Voluntary recruitment below 18 requires safeguards.
The Optional Protocol allowing children to file complaints to the Committee is the:|Third Optional Protocol (communications procedure)|First Optional Protocol|OPAC|Palermo Protocol|The OPIC (adopted 2011, in force 2014) allows individuals or groups of children to submit complaints about violations of their rights.
The Declaration of the Rights of the Child (non-binding) was adopted by the UN in:|1959|1924|1989|2000|It contains ten principles. The 1924 Geneva Declaration was drafted by Eglantyne Jebb and adopted by the League of Nations.
Universal Children's Day is observed on:|20 November|1 June|12 June|11 October|20 November commemorates both the 1959 Declaration and the 1989 Convention. 1 June is International Children's Day in many countries.
World Day Against Child Labour is observed on:|12 June|1 May|30 July|20 November|It was launched by the ILO in 2002. May Day (1 May) is Labour Day; 30 July is World Day against Trafficking in Persons.
International Day of the Girl Child is observed on:|11 October|8 March|25 November|10 December|The UN General Assembly designated 11 October in 2011. 8 March is International Women's Day.
World Mental Health Day is observed on:|10 October|7 April|5 June|1 December|10 October is World Mental Health Day (World Federation for Mental Health). World Health Day is 7 April.
Which Sustainable Development Goal target calls for ending abuse, exploitation, trafficking and violence against children?|SDG 16.2|SDG 1.1|SDG 7.1|SDG 13.2|SDG 16.2 is under 'Peace, Justice and Strong Institutions'. SDG 5.3 targets child marriage, SDG 8.7 child labour, and SDG 4 quality education.
SDG 5.3 aims to eliminate:|Child, early and forced marriage and FGM|Poverty|Hunger|Pollution|SDG 5 is Gender Equality. Target 5.3 eliminates harmful practices such as child marriage and female genital mutilation.
The UN Convention against Transnational Organized Crime supplement on human trafficking is called:|Palermo Protocol|Geneva Protocol|Kyoto Protocol|Montreal Protocol|The Protocol to Prevent, Suppress and Punish Trafficking in Persons, Especially Women and Children, adopted in 2000, defines trafficking.
The UN Standard Minimum Rules for the Administration of Juvenile Justice (1985) are known as the:|Beijing Rules|Havana Rules|Tokyo Rules|Riyadh Guidelines|Beijing Rules (1985); Riyadh Guidelines (1990) on prevention of delinquency; Havana Rules (1990) on juveniles deprived of liberty; Tokyo Rules (1990) on non-custodial measures.
The UN Guidelines for the Prevention of Juvenile Delinquency (1990) are called:|Riyadh Guidelines|Beijing Rules|Havana Rules|Paris Principles|The Riyadh Guidelines emphasize prevention through family, education and community involvement rather than punishment.
The UN Rules for the Protection of Juveniles Deprived of their Liberty are called:|Havana Rules|Beijing Rules|Mandela Rules|Bangkok Rules|Havana Rules (1990). Mandela Rules concern prisoners generally and Bangkok Rules concern women prisoners.
Malala Yousafzai shared the Nobel Peace Prize in 2014 with:|Kailash Satyarthi|Barack Obama|Muhammad Yunus|Abdul Sattar Edhi|Malala (b. 1997, Swat) and Kailash Satyarthi of India were honoured for struggle against suppression of children and young people and for the right to education. Malala remains the youngest Nobel laureate.
The first Special Representative of the UN Secretary-General on Violence against Children was:|Marta Santos Pais|Kofi Annan|Graça Machel|Ban Ki-moon|The post was established in 2009 following the UN Study on Violence Against Children (2006) led by Paulo Sérgio Pinheiro.
The Safe Schools Declaration (2015) aims to protect:|Education from attack during armed conflict|Child marriage|Child labour|Birth registration|It is a political commitment to protect students, teachers, schools and universities during conflict.
Which organization is the UN agency leading on international labour standards, including child labour?|ILO|WHO|FAO|UNESCO|The International Labour Organization, founded 1919, is a tripartite UN agency (government, employers, workers) and runs the IPEC programme on child labour.
The only legally binding UN treaty dedicated entirely to children's rights is the:|Convention on the Rights of the Child|Declaration of the Rights of the Child 1959|Universal Declaration of Human Rights|Geneva Declaration 1924|The 1924 and 1959 Declarations are non-binding statements. The UNCRC (1989) is a treaty that legally binds states parties and is monitored by the Committee on the Rights of the Child.
