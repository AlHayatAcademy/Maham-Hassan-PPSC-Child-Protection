# Test 8 - Sociology
The term 'sociology' was coined by:|Auguste Comte|Herbert Spencer|Karl Marx|Max Weber|Comte coined 'sociologie' in 1838 and is called the father of sociology. Spencer coined 'survival of the fittest'.
Auguste Comte's book on sociology is:|Positive Philosophy (Cours de philosophie positive)|The Division of Labour in Society|Das Kapital|Suicide|Comte proposed the Law of Three Stages: theological, metaphysical and positive (scientific).
Emile Durkheim is known for the study of:|Suicide and social facts|Capitalism only|Psychoanalysis|Primary groups|Durkheim wrote 'The Rules of Sociological Method', 'The Division of Labour in Society' (1893), 'Suicide' (1897) and 'Elementary Forms of Religious Life'.
Durkheim's four types of suicide are:|Egoistic, altruistic, anomic, fatalistic|Passive, active, silent, loud|Primary, secondary, tertiary, quaternary|Social, legal, moral, cultural|They arise from too little or too much social integration or regulation. Anomie refers to normlessness.
Karl Marx is associated with:|Class conflict and historical materialism|Symbolic interactionism|Structural functionalism|Social Darwinism|Marx wrote 'Das Kapital' and the 'Communist Manifesto' (with Engels, 1848). He viewed history as a struggle between bourgeoisie and proletariat.
Max Weber's famous book is:|The Protestant Ethic and the Spirit of Capitalism|Suicide|The Social Contract|The Wealth of Nations|Weber introduced verstehen (interpretive understanding), ideal types, bureaucracy and types of authority (traditional, charismatic, legal-rational).
Weber's three types of authority are:|Traditional, charismatic and legal-rational|Primary, secondary, tertiary|Mechanical, organic, anomic|Caste, class, status|Authority is legitimate power. Bureaucracy is the typical form of legal-rational authority.
Which sociologist developed the concept of 'looking-glass self'?|Charles Horton Cooley|G.H. Mead|Weber|Marx|Cooley said we see ourselves through others' reactions. G.H. Mead developed 'I' and 'Me' and symbolic interactionism.
Symbolic interactionism was developed by:|George Herbert Mead|Talcott Parsons|Karl Marx|Herbert Spencer|Interactionists study how meanings arise through social interaction, e.g., labelling theory.
Structural functionalism is associated with:|Talcott Parsons and Robert Merton|Marx|Weber|Mead|It views society as a system of interrelated parts functioning to maintain stability. Merton distinguished manifest and latent functions.
A group in which relations are face-to-face, intimate and personal is called a:|Primary group|Secondary group|Reference group|Out-group|Cooley described the family and play group as primary groups. Secondary groups are formal and impersonal, such as an office.
The concept of 'in-group' and 'out-group' was introduced by:|William Graham Sumner|Cooley|Mead|Durkheim|Sumner described 'we-feeling' of the in-group (and ethnocentrism). A reference group is one whose standards we use for self-evaluation.
Ferdinand Tönnies distinguished between:|Gemeinschaft and Gesellschaft|Mechanical and organic solidarity|Caste and class|Sacred and profane|Gemeinschaft is community with close ties; Gesellschaft is society with impersonal ties.
Culture is defined by E.B. Tylor as:|A complex whole including knowledge, belief, art, morals, law, custom and other habits|Only material things|Only language|Only religion|Tylor's definition (1871) is the classical one. Culture is learned, shared and transmitted.
The material aspect of culture such as tools and buildings is called:|Material culture|Non-material culture|Subculture|Counterculture|Non-material culture includes beliefs, values and norms.
The term 'culture lag' was introduced by:|William F. Ogburn|Durkheim|Comte|Weber|Ogburn said that non-material culture changes slower than material culture, e.g., laws may lag behind technology like the internet.
Folkways are:|Customary ways of everyday behaviour|Strict laws|Moral obligations|Taboos|Sumner classified norms as folkways, mores and laws. Mores are moral norms whose violation is serious, e.g., incest taboo.
The process through which a person learns culture and becomes a social being is:|Socialization|Stratification|Migration|Segregation|Primary socialization occurs in the family; secondary socialization occurs in school, peer groups and media. Agents of socialization influence values and behaviour.
The most important agent of primary socialization is:|Family|Media|Peers|Religion|The family provides early emotional care and teaches language and norms, hence child neglect has deep consequences.
Cases of isolated children like Anna and Isabelle showed the importance of:|Social contact for socialization|Genes|Intelligence tests|Climate|Kingsley Davis studied them and concluded that human nature develops only through social interaction.
The family consisting of husband, wife and unmarried children is called:|Nuclear family|Joint family|Extended family|Reconstituted family|A joint family includes several generations or brothers' families living together, common in rural South Asia.
Which type of family is typical in traditional Pakistani rural society?|Joint family|Single-parent family|Cohabitation|Communes|Joint families are changing with urbanization, migration and economic pressures toward nuclear families.
Marriage between members of the same group is called:|Endogamy|Exogamy|Monogamy|Polyandry|Exogamy is marriage outside the group. Polygyny: more wives; polyandry: more husbands; monogamy: one spouse.
Matrilocal residence means a couple lives with:|The wife's family|The husband's family|Alone|In a hotel|Patrilocal is the norm in Pakistan. Neolocal means couple establishes a new household.
The system of social stratification based on birth and ascribed status is:|Caste system|Class system|Estate system|Slavery|Caste is closed and hereditary. Class is open and based on economic position and achievements.
'Social mobility' means:|Movement between social positions|Moving homes|Travel|Trade|Vertical mobility is upward or downward in status; horizontal mobility is a change of occupation without status change.
The concept of 'social capital' emphasizes:|Networks, trust and norms that facilitate cooperation|Bank money|Land|Gold|Pierre Bourdieu, James Coleman and Robert Putnam developed the concept.
Social control refers to:|Mechanisms to regulate behaviour and ensure conformity|Government taxes|Population control|Control of prices|Formal controls include laws and police; informal controls include family, gossip and customs.
Deviance means:|Behaviour that violates social norms|Following norms|Intelligence|Charity|Deviance is relative to culture and time. Crime is deviance that violates criminal law.
The functionalist who considered some deviance as normal and even functional in society is:|Emile Durkheim|Cooley|Mead|Weber|Durkheim said crime is normal because it appears in all societies and may clarify moral boundaries.
Merton's strain theory explains deviance due to:|Gap between cultural goals and institutional means|Genetics|Climate|Intelligence|Merton's modes of adaptation: conformity, innovation, ritualism, retreatism and rebellion.
Labelling theory is associated with:|Howard Becker|Merton|Comte|Spencer|Becker's 'Outsiders' (1963) argues that deviance is created by society's reaction; labelling a child 'bad' may lead to a deviant identity.
Edwin Sutherland proposed the theory of:|Differential association|Anomie|Strain|Broken windows|Criminal behaviour is learned through interaction with others who favour law violation.
The phenomenon in which a migrating group adopts the culture of the host society is:|Assimilation|Segregation|Diffusion|Conflict|Acculturation is cultural change from contact; assimilation is the complete absorption.
Rapid growth of cities and the shift of population from rural to urban areas is:|Urbanization|Industrialization|Globalization|Modernization|Pakistan is one of the most urbanizing countries in South Asia with around 38% urban population (2023 census).
Slums and 'katchi abadis' are mainly associated with:|Rapid urbanization and poverty|Rural prosperity|Low population|Good planning|Poor housing, child labour and lack of services expose children to risk.
Poverty in which a person cannot meet basic needs is called:|Absolute poverty|Relative poverty|Mobility|Marginality|Relative poverty is defined in relation to the standard of living in a society. Pakistan uses a cost-of-basic-needs approach.
The Multidimensional Poverty Index (MPI) includes:|Health, education and living standards|Only income|Only land|Only age|It was developed by Oxford (OPHI) and UNDP and shows overlapping deprivations.
The term 'social exclusion' refers to:|Denying individuals or groups access to resources and participation|Giving charity|Group parties|Voting|Children with disabilities, minorities and street children face exclusion.
A sudden mass movement of public concern is called:|Social movement|Constitution|Census|Treaty|Social movements aim to bring about or resist change, e.g., the women's rights movement and Lawyers' movement.
Gender is best defined as:|Socially constructed roles and expectations for men and women|Biological sex|Age|Race|Sex is biological; gender is cultural. Gender discrimination affects girls' education and early marriage.
'Patriarchy' means:|A social system dominated by men|Rule by women|Rule by elders|Rule by youth|Patriarchal norms underlie preference for sons, honour crimes and restrictions on girls.
Sociological imagination was introduced by:|C. Wright Mills|Comte|Marx|Durkheim|Mills (1959) said it links personal troubles with public issues of social structure.
The branch of sociology that studies population is:|Demography|Ecology|Criminology|Anthropology|Demographers study fertility, mortality and migration. Pakistan's growth rate in 2023 was about 2.55% per year.
Pakistan's population according to the 2023 digital census was approximately:|241.5 million|207.7 million|130 million|300 million|Total 241.49 million with annual growth 2.55%. The 2017 census recorded 207.68 million.
Dependency ratio is the ratio of:|Dependents (children, elderly) to working-age population|Males to females|Urban to rural|Rich to poor|A high child dependency ratio demands investment in education and health.
Ethnocentrism means:|Judging other cultures by one's own standards|Respecting all cultures|Worshiping ancestors|Learning languages|Cultural relativism says each culture should be understood on its own terms.
Anomie, a state of normlessness, is a concept of:|Durkheim|Mead|Cooley|Weber|It occurs during rapid social change or crisis when old norms break down.
The term 'mechanical solidarity' refers to:|Social cohesion based on similarity in traditional societies|Cohesion based on division of labour|Machine work|Chaos|Durkheim said modern societies show organic solidarity based on interdependence.
Which research method is used to study 'a community through long-term observation by living in it'?|Participant observation|Survey|Experiment|Census|Ethnography and participant observation are common in sociology and anthropology.
