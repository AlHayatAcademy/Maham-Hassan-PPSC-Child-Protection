# Test 2 - Punjab Destitute and Neglected Children Act 2004: Sections 24-51 (verify against latest amendments)
Under Section 24, a Child Protection Officer who takes a destitute and neglected child into custody must produce the child before the Court within:|Twenty-four hours|Forty-eight hours|Seven days|One month|Section 24: the CPO may take the child into custody and shall produce the child before the Court within twenty-four hours. This is one of the most frequently asked items.
Section 24 (Rescue) deals with:|Taking a destitute and neglected child into custody by a Child Protection Officer|Fund audit|Board meetings|Transfer to other provinces|Part VII (Sections 24-33) covers rescue, custody, care and rehabilitation.
If the child is with a parent or guardian, the CPO under the first proviso to Section 24:|Does not take custody but first reports to the Court|Always arrests the parent|Closes the file|Sends the child abroad|The Court then follows Section 26 to summon the parent or guardian to produce the child and show cause.
The first proviso to Section 24 does NOT apply to a child who is:|Found begging or the victim of an offence by the parent or guardian|Orphaned|Sick|Disabled|In these cases the CPO may take the child into custody directly, because leaving the child with the parent or guardian is unsafe.
Under Section 25, if a child cannot be produced immediately before the Court, the child is taken to:|The nearest child protection institution for temporary custody|A police lockup|The officer's home|A jail|Section 25: the child must be produced immediately; otherwise temporary custody in the nearest institution until production.
Section 26 empowers the Court to:|Summon the parent or guardian to produce the child and show cause, and issue a search warrant if the child may be hidden|Fine the child|Dismiss the CPO|Issue passports|The Court may order admission to an institution or allow the child to stay with the parent on suitable surety.
Under Section 27, the Court examines on oath:|The person who brought the child or made the report|The child only|The Home Secretary|Nobody|Section 27: the Court records the substance of the examination and may order admission of the child pending inquiry.
Section 28 empowers the Court, if the child is destitute and neglected, to order:|Admission to a child protection institution or custody with a suitable person|Imprisonment of the child|Marriage of the child|Adoption abroad|Custody lasts until the child attains eighteen years or for a shorter period in exceptional cases.
Under Section 28, the Court may require the person given custody to execute:|A bond, with or without sureties, for care, education and welfare of the child|A sale deed|A passport|A will|The Court may also order periodic welfare reports through an authorized officer and compel production of the child at any time.
If a bond condition is breached, the Court may:|Order admission to an institution or transfer to another suitable person|Fine the child|Close the case|Give the child to the informer|Section 28 allows the Court to modify custody on breach of conditions.
Section 29 concerns a child who:|Resides outside the local jurisdiction of the Court|Is above 18|Is a foreigner|Is a juvenile offender|The Court may direct that the child be kept and produced before the Court having jurisdiction over the child's residence, which treats the child as produced under Section 24.
Section 30 deals with:|Warrant to search for a destitute and neglected child|Fund accounts|Board meetings|Transfers|On sworn information by a person acting in the child's interest, the Court may issue a warrant; the officer may be accompanied by the informant, a medical practitioner or police.
Under Section 31, a person who gives false, frivolous or vexatious information may be ordered to pay compensation up to:|Rs 50,000|Rs 5,000|Rs 1,00,000|Rs 10,000|Section 31(1). The informer must first be heard. Payment cannot be made until one month after the order.
Under Section 31, default of payment of compensation may result in simple imprisonment up to:|Six months|One year|One month|Two years|Section 31(3).
Compensation under Section 31 cannot be paid until ___ after the order.|One month|One week|Six months|One year|Section 31(5). This allows time for any challenge.
Section 32 provides that a child shall remain in institutional custody until the age of:|Eighteen years|Fifteen years|Twenty-one years|Fourteen years|Section 32 (subject to Court orders or Bureau directions). Section 28 also says custody is until age 18 or shorter in exceptional cases.
Under Section 33 the Court may order a parent to pay:|Maintenance for the child at a rate fixed by the Court considering the parent's ability|A fine to the government|Travelling expenses|Nothing|Section 33. Non-payment is recoverable as arrears of land revenue.
Maintenance arrears payable by a parent under Section 33 are recoverable as:|Arrears of land revenue|Income tax|Penalty under PPC|Court fee|Section 33(2).
Part VIII of the Act (Sections 34-43) deals with:|Special offences|Rescue|The Bureau|The Fund|Offences against children carry imprisonment up to 3 years and/or fines up to Rs 50,000 (original text), though a 2025 amendment bill proposes stricter penalties.
Section 34 punishes:|Unauthorized custody of a destitute and neglected child|Begging|Employing a clerk|Selling land|Penalty: imprisonment up to 3 years, or fine up to Rs 50,000, or both.
Section 35 punishes:|Cruelty to children by a non-parent having charge or control (ill-treatment, neglect, abandonment, exposure)|Child marriage|Child begging only|Theft|Penalty up to 3 years or fine up to Rs 50,000 or both. A proviso says lawful punishment for good and sufficient reason is not an offence.
Section 36 punishes:|Employing or causing a child to beg|Employing a child in a factory|Child marriage|Child trafficking|Penalty up to 3 years, or fine up to Rs 50,000, or both. Connivance or encouragement is also punished.
Section 37 punishes giving a child any intoxicant or narcotic drug without the prescription of:|A qualified medical practitioner|A teacher|A parent|A police officer|Penalty up to 3 years, or fine up to Rs 50,000, or both. The Act also says the amount is 'fifty thousand rupees'.
The punishment under Section 38 (allowing a child in places selling liquor or drugs) is:|Fine up to Rs 50,000|Imprisonment of 5 years|Death|Imprisonment of 3 years|Section 38 punishes taking a child to such a place, permitting entry (owner or manager) or causing the child to go there. The penalty is a fine only in the original text.
Section 39 punishes inciting a child to bet or wager. The maximum imprisonment is:|Six months|Three years|Five years|One year|Section 39: up to six months, or fine up to Rs 50,000, or both.
Section 40 punishes exposing a child to:|Seduction, sodomy, prostitution or other immoral conditions after obtaining custody on a pretext|Smoking only|School absence|Hard work|Penalty up to 3 years, or fine up to Rs 50,000, or both.
Section 41 punishes a person who:|Helps a child to escape from an institution or lawful custody, or harbours such a child|Teaches a child|Feeds a child|Gives a child a job|Penalty up to five years, or fine up to Rs 50,000, or both. This is the longest imprisonment in Part VIII.
Which offence under Part VIII carries the longest maximum imprisonment (five years)?|Section 41 (abetting escape)|Section 34|Section 36|Section 39|Section 41: up to 5 years. Sections 34-37 and 40 carry up to 3 years; Section 39 six months; Section 38 fine only.
Which Part VIII section carries a fine only (no imprisonment) in the original text?|Section 38|Section 36|Section 41|Section 34|Section 38: fine up to Rs 50,000 on a person who takes or permits a child to enter a place selling liquor or drugs.
Under Section 42, all offences under Part VIII are:|Cognizable|Non-cognizable|Compoundable only|Civil|Section 42. A few past-paper keys list 'cognizable and non-bailable', so if an option 'Both' appears, check the latest official wording.
Under Section 43, offences under the Act are investigated, tried and punished according to the:|Code of Criminal Procedure 1898|Civil Procedure Code|Qanun-e-Shahadat|Police Rules only|Section 43. The Court itself is a special court for rescue and custody matters, not for trial of offences.
Part IX of the Act (Sections 44-51) is titled:|Miscellaneous|Offences|Courts|Bureau|It covers discharge, transfer, public servants, protection of acts in good faith, overriding effect, rules, regulations and repeal.
Under Section 44, the Bureau may discharge a child from an institution:|At any time, absolutely or on conditions|Only after 18|Only with Governor's consent|Never|Section 44. The Court may also discharge a child from an institution or from the custody of a suitable person at any stage.
Under Section 45, a child should normally be kept in an institution:|At or nearest to the child's domicile|In Lahore only|In any other province|Abroad|Section 45. The Bureau may transfer a child to another institution within Punjab after consulting the manager.
A child may be transferred to an institution in another province with:|That province's consent and a similar law there|The Prime Minister's order|Federal Court order|No consent|Section 45: Government may transfer a child to an institution in another province only if there is similar law and consent.
Under Section 46, officers appointed or authorized under the Act are deemed to be:|Public servants within the meaning of Section 21 of the Pakistan Penal Code|Private persons|Police officers|Judges|Section 46. Obstructing them can attract criminal liability.
Under Section 47 no suit, prosecution or legal proceeding lies against a person for anything done:|In good faith for the welfare of a child under the Act|Without authority|In bad faith|For personal gain|Section 47 (protection) encourages officers and informants to act without fear of litigation.
Under Section 48, the Act:|Overrides any conflicting law|Is overridden by other laws|Is a suggestion|Applies only to Lahore|Section 48 gives the Act overriding effect over inconsistent provisions of other laws.
Rules under the Act are made by:|The Government by notification|The Bureau|The Court|The Chairperson|Section 49. Regulations are framed by the Bureau under Section 50 and must be consistent with the Act and rules.
Regulations under the Act are made by:|The Bureau|The Government|The High Court|The Chief Minister|Section 50. Compare with Section 49 (rules by Government).
Section 51 of the Act repeals which law?|The Punjab Children Ordinance 1983, the Punjab Youthful Offenders Ordinance 1983 and the Punjab Supervision and Control of Children Homes Act 1976|The Pakistan Penal Code|The Police Order 2002|The Guardians and Wards Act 1890|Existing children's homes under the 1976 Act are deemed child protection institutions.
Existing children's homes under the Punjab Supervision and Control of Children Homes Act 1976 are deemed after the PDNCA to be:|Child protection institutions|Courts|Prisons|Private schools|Section 51 saves earlier actions and institutions so far as they are consistent with the new Act.
Part IV of the Act deals with:|The Destitute and Neglected Children's Welfare Fund|Courts|Offences|Definitions|Part IV (Sections 18-19). Section 19 says the Fund is audited as prescribed and the annual audit report is made public.
The annual audit report of the Fund must be:|Made public by the Bureau and each institution|Kept secret|Burned|Sent abroad|Section 19 transparency requirement.
Under Section 20, the Bureau may delegate management of a child protection institution to:|A local government or a non-governmental organization on terms fixed by the Board|Any private person|A foreign government|The Police|The Bureau may also recognize other public or NGO-run institutions and establish aftercare associations.
Section 20 allows the Bureau to give orders to fix management of an institution that breaches rules, but only after:|Hearing the existing management|Informing the media|Court approval|A vote|Section 20(7) gives natural justice protection.
Which Section provides that child protection institutions are managed as prescribed?|Section 21|Section 5|Section 31|Section 45|Section 21 (Administration).
Part VII of the Act is titled:|Rescue, Custody, Care, Protection and Rehabilitation of Destitute and Neglected Children|Special Offences|The Fund|Courts|Part VII includes Sections 24-33.
