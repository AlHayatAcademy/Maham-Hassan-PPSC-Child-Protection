# Test 1 - Punjab Destitute and Neglected Children Act 2004: Sections 1-23 (text as originally enacted and amended in 2007; later amendments in 2014/2017/2025 may differ - verify on punjablaws.gov.pk)
The short title of the Act is:|The Punjab Destitute and Neglected Children Act 2004|The Punjab Children Protection Act 2004|The Punjab Child Welfare Ordinance 2004|The Punjab Juvenile Justice Act 2004|Section 1 gives the short title as the Punjab Destitute and Neglected Children Act 2004 (PDNCA). It consolidates the law on rescue, protective custody, care and rehabilitation of destitute and neglected children.
The Act extends to:|The whole of the Punjab|The whole of Pakistan|Lahore only|Islamabad Capital Territory|Section 1 says it extends to the whole of the Punjab. It comes into force at once, but applies to areas and from dates notified by Government.
Under Section 2 (Saving), the Government may by notification:|Exempt any class of children or any area from all or any provisions of the Act|Repeal the Act|Transfer the Bureau to Federal Government|Abolish the courts|Section 2 allows exemption of any class of children or any area from the Act by notification in the official Gazette.
The preamble of the Act excludes children who are:|Involved in criminal litigation|Orphans|Below 5 years|Disabled|The Act deals with destitute and neglected children and is not meant for children in conflict with the law, who are covered by juvenile justice laws.
In the Act 'Government' means:|The Government of the Punjab in the Home Department|The Federal Government|The Local Government Department|The High Court|Section 3 defines Government as the Punjab Government, Home Department. This is why the Bureau is linked to the Home Department.
In the Act, 'child' means a natural person who has not attained the age of:|Eighteen years|Fifteen years|Sixteen years|Fourteen years|Section 3(e). However, Section 4 restricts initiation of Part VII proceedings to a child under fifteen.
In the Act, 'Code' means the:|Code of Criminal Procedure 1898|Code of Civil Procedure 1908|Pakistan Penal Code 1860|Qanun-e-Shahadat Order 1984|Section 3(i). Note that Section 23 says the Code and Qanun-e-Shahadat Order do not apply to the Court unless the Act or rules say so.
In the Act, 'Court' means:|The Child Protection Court or any court exercising its powers under the Act|The Supreme Court|The Family Court|The Magistrate only|Section 3(j) defines Court as the Child Protection Court established under Section 22 and any court exercising its powers.
In the Act, 'Bureau' means the:|Child Protection and Welfare Bureau established under Section 5|Anti-Corruption Establishment|Punjab Social Welfare Board|Punjab Police Bureau|Section 3(d). The Bureau is a body corporate with perpetual succession.
In the Act, 'Board' means the:|Board of Governors constituted under Section 6|Board of Revenue|Board of Intermediate Education|Punjab Education Board|Section 3(b). Executive authority and management of the Bureau vests in this Board (Section 10).
In the Act, 'Fund' means the:|Destitute and Neglected Children's Welfare Fund established under Section 18|Zakat Fund|Child Education Fund|Chief Minister's Relief Fund|Section 3(l). The Bureau and each child protection institution shall establish such a Fund under Section 18.
According to Section 3, 'begging' includes:|Soliciting or receiving alms, and displaying wounds or deformities to get alms|Only street singing|Only door-to-door selling|Only sitting outside a mosque|Section 3(a) covers soliciting or receiving alms in a public place, exposing sores, wounds or deformity to get alms and wandering without visible means of subsistence.
In the Act, 'brothel' means:|A place used for prostitution or known to offer it|A hostel|A children's home|A theatre|Section 3(c). A child living in a brothel or with a prostitute is a destitute and neglected child under Section 3(k)(iv).
In the Act, 'narcotic drug' has the meaning given in the:|Control of Narcotic Substances Act 1997|Pakistan Penal Code|Drugs Act 1976|Customs Act 1969|Section 3(p) refers to the Control of Narcotic Substances Act 1997. Section 37 punishes giving an intoxicant or narcotic to a child without a doctor's prescription.
In the Act, 'local area' means a district, city district, tehsil or town as defined in the:|Punjab Local Government Ordinance 2001|Punjab Municipal Act 1911|Land Revenue Act 1967|Police Order 2002|Section 3(n). Courts and child protection units are established for a local area.
In the Act, 'prostitution' means:|Illegal sexual intercourse for hire, in money or kind|Any dance|Any marriage|Any gathering|Section 3(r). Exposing a child to prostitution is punishable under Section 40.
Section 3(k) lists how many circumstances in which a child is 'destitute and neglected'?|Eight|Five|Three|Ten|The eight circumstances include begging, no home or means, unfit guardian, living in a brothel, abuse or exploitation, beyond parental control, loss of parents without income, and victim of an offence by a parent or guardian.
A child who is found begging is:|A destitute and neglected child under Section 3(k)(i)|Not covered by the Act|A juvenile offender only|A ward of court|Begging is the first circumstance listed. For such a child a CPO need not first report to the Court even if with a parent (second proviso to Section 24).
A child who has lost one or both parents and has no adequate means is:|Destitute and neglected under Section 3(k)(vii)|Adopted|A ward only|Under no category|Orphanhood plus lack of adequate income qualifies. A child with lost parents but with adequate income does not qualify under this clause.
A child who is beyond parental control is covered under which clause of Section 3(k)?|(vi)|(i)|(iii)|(viii)|Clause (vi) is 'is beyond parental control'. Clause (iii) is unfit or incapacitated parent or guardian.
A child who is a victim of an offence in which the parent or guardian is convicted or accused falls under:|Section 3(k)(viii)|Section 3(k)(ii)|Section 3(k)(iv)|Section 3(k)(vi)|This is the last circumstance in the list, which protects children whose own guardians are accused or convicted of an offence against them.
'Suitable person' in the Act means:|A person fit to take custody of a destitute and neglected child|A judge|A police officer|A donor|Section 3(t). Court may place a child with a suitable person under Section 28.
Under Section 4, proceedings under Part VII can be initiated for a child who has not attained the age of:|Fifteen years|Eighteen years|Twelve years|Ten years|Section 4(1): if the child reaches fifteen during proceedings, the proceedings continue. This is a favourite exam question because it differs from the 18-year definition of child.
Under Section 4, who decides a dispute about the age of a child?|The Court|The police|The Bureau|The Chief Minister|Section 4(2): the Court decides the age question first, before proceeding.
The Child Protection and Welfare Bureau is established under which Section?|Section 5|Section 3|Section 9|Section 22|Section 5(1): Government shall establish the Bureau as soon as possible. Part II (ss.5-14) is about the Bureau.
Under Section 5 the Bureau is a:|Body corporate with perpetual succession|Department of Home|Court|Political party|It can hold property, sue and be sued. Government may transfer land or buildings to it free of cost.
Who is the Patron-in-Chief of the Board of Governors?|The Chief Minister|The Governor|The Chief Justice|The Home Secretary|Under Section 6 (as substituted in 2007), the Chief Minister is Patron-in-Chief; the Chairperson is appointed from among the members.
Which of the following is NOT a member of the Board of Governors?|The Prime Minister|Secretary Home Department|Secretary Social Welfare Department|Secretary Local Government Department|The Board includes the Chief Minister, three secretaries (Home, Social Welfare, Local Government), MPAs, donor representatives, professors, non-official members and the Director General.
How many Members of the Provincial Assembly are on the Board?|Three (two from treasury benches and one from the Opposition)|Five|One|Seven|One MPA is nominated by the Leader of the Opposition.
The term of the representatives of donors on the Board is:|Four years|Two years|Five years|Three years|Donors' representatives (two) and the six non-official members serve four-year terms.
How many non-official members does the Board have?|Six|Two|Four|Ten|Each non-official member must hold at least a Master's degree and have five years' field experience in child welfare or related fields.
The quorum for a Board meeting is:|Five members including two ex-officio members|Three members|Seven members|Ten members|Section 6(7): the presence of five members, including two ex-officio, is needed. Decisions are by majority with a casting vote to the person presiding.
Who has a casting vote in case of a tie in the Board?|The person presiding the meeting|The Director General|The Home Secretary|Nobody|Decisions are by majority of members present and voting. In a tie the presiding person has a second or casting vote.
The Chairperson of the Board is appointed by:|The Government from among the members|The High Court|The Director General|Donors|The Chairperson can serve a maximum of two consecutive terms and holds office at the pleasure of Government.
Who is the Member/Secretary of the Board?|The Director General of the Bureau|The Home Secretary|The Chairperson|The Chief Minister|The Director General is also the chief executive of the Bureau (Section 13).
Non-ex-officio members of the Board hold office at the pleasure of:|The Government|The Court|The Director General|The Chief Minister's wife|Section 7: Government may remove them as prescribed; they may resign by writing to Government.
Which of the following is a power of the Board under Section 9?|To establish the Fund and supervise prosecutions under the Act|To try offenders|To pass death sentences|To appoint judges|The Board also establishes, manages and recognizes child protection institutions, appoints staff and authorizes expenditure from the Fund.
Under Section 10, executive authority and management of the Bureau vests in the:|Board|Government|Director General|Court|This is a repeated exam question. The Director General is the chief executive but the executive authority vests in the Board.
The Director General of the Bureau is appointed by:|Government for a specified term|The Chief Justice|The Board|The Governor|Section 13: the DG is the chief executive and can be removed by Government as prescribed; he or she may resign to Government.
The annual progress report of the Bureau is prepared by the:|Director General|Chairperson|Chief Minister|Court|Section 14: the DG prepares the report after each financial year and presents it at the next Board meeting.
Child Protection Officers are appointed by the:|Bureau|High Court|Police|Local Government|Section 15(1). The Board determines eligibility, terms and conditions of service.
Who must provide assistance to a Child Protection Officer when asked?|The Station House Officer of the police station|The Deputy Commissioner|The Judge|The Governor|Section 15: a CPO may seek police help and the SHO shall provide appropriate assistance.
Part III of the Act deals with:|Child Protection Officers and Child Protection Unit|Courts|Fund|Offences|Sections 15-17. A unit is established for each local area under Section 16 and works under the control and supervision of the Bureau (Section 17).
Child Protection Courts are provided in Part ___ of the Act.|VI|II|VIII|IX|Part VI (Sections 22-23) deals with Child Protection Courts. Part VII covers rescue, custody and rehabilitation. NOTE: some past-paper keys show VII, so verify against the latest official text.
The presiding officer of a Child Protection Court is appointed by:|Government in consultation with the Lahore High Court|Chief Minister alone|Bureau|Board|Section 22(2). The High Court may prescribe qualifications.
Until a Court is established for a local area, the Lahore High Court may confer its powers on a:|Sessions Judge or Additional Sessions Judge|Civil Judge|Magistrate only|Chief Justice|Section 22(3). This is why Sessions Courts function as Child Protection Courts in many districts.
Under Section 23 the Court shall finally decide a case within ___ of the production of the child before it.|One month|One week|Six months|One year|Section 23(2) of the original text. A few past-paper keys show 'three months', which could reflect later amendments, so check the latest official Act before the exam.
Which of the following does NOT apply to the Child Protection Court unless the Act or rules say so?|The Code of Criminal Procedure and the Qanun-e-Shahadat Order 1984|The Constitution|The Penal Code|Islamic injunctions|Section 23(3). The Court follows a simplified child-friendly procedure.
An appeal or revision against an order of the Child Protection Court:|Does not lie, but the Court may recall or modify its orders in the interest of justice|Lies to the Supreme Court|Lies to the Board|Lies to the Governor|Section 23(4). This is a typical exam question.
The Child Protection Court is deemed to be a Court of Sessions under Section ___ of the Code.|476|144|154|497|Section 23(5) deems the Court a Court of Sessions for section 476 of the Code of Criminal Procedure.
