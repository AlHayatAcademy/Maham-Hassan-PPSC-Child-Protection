# Test 17 - English (PPSC pattern)
Choose the word nearest in meaning to BENEVOLENT:|Kind|Cruel|Selfish|Lazy|Benevolent means well-meaning and generous. Related word: benefactor (one who helps others). Antonym: malevolent.
Choose the word nearest in meaning to CANDID:|Frank|Secretive|Rude|Shy|Candid means truthful and straightforward. The noun is candour.
Choose the word nearest in meaning to ABUNDANT:|Plentiful|Scarce|Rare|Little|Abundant = existing in large quantities. Antonym: scarce or meagre.
Choose the word nearest in meaning to DILIGENT:|Hardworking|Careless|Lazy|Sleepy|Diligence is steady, earnest effort. Antonym: indolent.
Choose the word nearest in meaning to EMINENT:|Distinguished|Ordinary|Hidden|Weak|Eminent describes a famous and respected person. Do not confuse with imminent (about to happen) or immanent.
Choose the word nearest in meaning to VAGUE:|Unclear|Precise|Sharp|Bright|Vague = not clearly expressed. Antonym: definite.
Choose the word nearest in meaning to MELANCHOLY:|Sadness|Joy|Anger|Hope|Melancholy is a pensive sadness. Related word: gloomy.
Choose the word nearest in meaning to PRAGMATIC:|Practical|Dreamy|Theoretical|Lazy|A pragmatic approach is based on practical results rather than theory.
Choose the word opposite in meaning to GENEROUS:|Stingy|Kind|Liberal|Noble|Generous = ready to give. Stingy or miserly = unwilling to spend or give.
Choose the word opposite in meaning to ANCIENT:|Modern|Old|Historic|Archaic|Ancient means very old; modern or contemporary is its antonym.
Choose the word opposite in meaning to TRANSPARENT:|Opaque|Clear|Visible|Glassy|Transparent allows light to pass through; opaque does not. Also used for honest and open governance.
Choose the word opposite in meaning to OPTIMISTIC:|Pessimistic|Hopeful|Cheerful|Confident|An optimist expects the best; a pessimist expects the worst.
Choose the word opposite in meaning to EXPAND:|Contract|Grow|Spread|Enlarge|Expand = increase in size; contract = decrease.
Choose the word opposite in meaning to SCARCE:|Plentiful|Rare|Limited|Few|Scarce = in short supply. Plentiful and abundant are opposites.
Choose the word opposite in meaning to HUMBLE:|Arrogant|Modest|Meek|Polite|Humble persons do not boast. Arrogant persons overrate themselves.
The idiom 'to break the ice' means:|To start a conversation in an awkward situation|To destroy something|To feel cold|To cancel a plan|It is used to ease tension when people meet for the first time.
The idiom 'a piece of cake' means:|Something very easy|A dessert|Something expensive|A difficult task|The exam was a piece of cake means it was easy.
The idiom 'once in a blue moon' means:|Very rarely|Every night|Daily|Frequently|It refers to a rare occurrence.
The idiom 'to burn the midnight oil' means:|To work or study late into the night|To waste fuel|To start a fire|To sleep early|It comes from oil lamps used for night-time work.
The idiom 'to cry over spilt milk' means:|To regret what cannot be undone|To be happy|To waste milk|To feel hungry|It advises against grieving over past mistakes.
The idiom 'to hit the nail on the head' means:|To say or do exactly the right thing|To work as a carpenter|To injure oneself|To make a mistake|It describes a precise, accurate remark.
The idiom 'a blessing in disguise' means:|A good thing that seemed bad at first|A secret blessing from a saint|A costume|A curse|It is used when a misfortune turns out beneficial.
The idiom 'to beat around the bush' means:|To avoid the main point|To hunt birds|To clean a garden|To run fast|It means speaking indirectly without coming to the point.
The idiom 'to bite the bullet' means:|To face a difficult situation bravely|To eat metal|To shoot someone|To refuse|It comes from the practice of biting a bullet during surgery without anaesthesia.
The phrase 'under the weather' means:|Feeling slightly ill|Standing in rain|Very happy|Rich|It is a common idiom for mild illness.
One who loves books is called a:|Bibliophile|Philanthropist|Misanthrope|Philatelist|Bibliophile = book lover; philatelist collects stamps; philanthropist loves mankind; misanthrope hates mankind.
Fear of heights is called:|Acrophobia|Claustrophobia|Hydrophobia|Xenophobia|Claustrophobia is fear of closed spaces; hydrophobia is fear of water; xenophobia is fear of foreigners.
One who knows everything is:|Omniscient|Omnipotent|Omnipresent|Optimist|Omnipotent = all-powerful; omnipresent = present everywhere.
The study of birds is called:|Ornithology|Entomology|Zoology|Herpetology|Entomology is the study of insects; herpetology is the study of reptiles and amphibians.
A medicine that cures all diseases is a:|Panacea|Antidote|Placebo|Vaccine|Panacea = universal remedy. Antidote counteracts poison.
Something lasting for a very short time is:|Ephemeral|Eternal|Perennial|Permanent|Ephemeral comes from Greek for 'lasting a day'.
Murder of a king is called:|Regicide|Homicide|Suicide|Genocide|Patricide is killing a father; fratricide killing a brother; infanticide killing an infant.
Fill in the blank: He ___ to school every day.|goes|go|going|gone|The simple present uses the verb with 's' for the third person singular subject.
Fill in the blank: Neither of the boys ___ present.|was|were|are|have been|'Neither' takes a singular verb. Similarly 'each' and 'either' are followed by singular verbs.
Fill in the blank: She has been living here ___ 2015.|since|for|from|by|'Since' is used for a point of time; 'for' is used for a duration (for ten years).
Fill in the blank: I look forward to ___ you soon.|seeing|see|saw|have seen|'To' in 'look forward to' is a preposition, so a gerund (-ing form) follows.
Fill in the blank: She is ___ honest girl.|an|a|the|no article|Use 'an' before a vowel sound; 'honest' begins with a silent h, so a vowel sound.
Fill in the blank: He is good ___ mathematics.|at|in|on|for|Good at (a subject or skill); interested in; fond of; afraid of.
Fill in the blank: She was accused ___ theft.|of|for|with|by|Accused of; charged with; suspected of.
Change into passive voice: 'The teacher praised the students.'|The students were praised by the teacher.|The students are praised by the teacher.|The students had praised the teacher.|The teacher was praised by students.|Past simple active becomes 'was/were + past participle' in the passive.
Change into indirect speech: He said, 'I am happy.'|He said that he was happy.|He said that I was happy.|He said that he is happy.|He told that he was happy.|In reported speech the present tense shifts to the past tense and the pronoun changes accordingly.
Choose the correctly spelt word:|Accommodation|Acommodation|Accomodation|Acomodation|Accommodation has double c and double m. Other tricky spellings: necessary, occurrence, separate, definitely.
The plural of 'phenomenon' is:|Phenomena|Phenomenons|Phenomenas|Phenomenum|Other Latin/Greek plurals: criterion–criteria; datum–data; analysis–analyses; crisis–crises.
The past participle of 'write' is:|Written|Wrote|Writed|Writing|Write–wrote–written. Similarly: go–went–gone; see–saw–seen.
In the sentence 'Honesty is the best policy', the word 'Honesty' is a/an:|Abstract noun|Proper noun|Collective noun|Adjective|Abstract nouns name qualities or ideas that cannot be touched, such as love, courage and honesty.
In the sentence 'She sings beautifully', the word 'beautifully' is a/an:|Adverb|Adjective|Noun|Verb|Adverbs modify verbs, adjectives or other adverbs and often end in -ly.
Choose the correct sentence:|Each of the students has completed the task.|Each of the students have completed the task.|Each of the students are completing the tasks.|Each of the student has completed the task.|'Each' is singular so it takes 'has'.
Choose the word nearest in meaning to RESILIENT:|Able to recover quickly|Fragile|Careless|Hostile|A resilient child bounces back from hardship. Resilience is a key concept in child protection and psychology.
Choose the word opposite in meaning to BRIEF:|Lengthy|Short|Concise|Quick|Brief means short in duration or words; lengthy or prolonged is the opposite.
Choose the word nearest in meaning to ENORMOUS:|Huge|Tiny|Narrow|Weak|Enormous = very large.
