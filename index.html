PPSC pattern & what to expect

Built from publicly reported third-party summaries of PPSC papers (the official PDFs could not be opened). Treat all of it as informed prediction, not inside information. Check the official syllabus for this post on ppsc.gop.pk.

Reported format

  • One paper, about 100 MCQs, 90 minutes, four options, reported −0.25 for each wrong answer and about 40% passing marks.
  • Syllabus listed in the advertisement (secondary source): Pakistan Studies, Current Affairs, Geography, Basic Maths, English, Urdu, Everyday Science, Basic Computer Skills, plus Islamic Studies for Muslims.

How PPSC questions behave

  • Straight factual recall: who, when, where, which. Few application or reasoning items.
  • Favourite topics: firsts and superlatives, international organisations, constitutional history and amendments, MS Office shortcuts, Urdu poets/books/proverbs, English idioms, antonyms, voice and narration, prepositions, short numeric maths.
  • Look-alike distractors and an occasional 'None of these'. Static facts matter far more than current events.
  • Negative marking: skip only when you cannot eliminate at least two options.

8-day plan

  • Days 1–2: Act tests 1–3, then Child Rights & Law. Memorise section numbers, ages, penalties and the Board composition.
  • Days 3–5: Pakistan Studies, Current Affairs, GK/Geography. Highest weight in the real paper.
  • Day 6: English, Urdu, Maths, Computer, Science drills.
  • Day 7: Social Sciences + Islamic Studies; redo every question you got wrong.
  • Day 8: One simulator only, light revision, sleep early.

Known conflicts in sources (verify before the exam)

  • Child Protection Court time limit: one month (Act text) vs three months (some keys).
  • Courts part: Part VI (Act text) vs Part VII (some keys).
  • Offences: "cognizable" (s.42) vs "cognizable and non-bailable" (some keys).
  • The Act was amended (2007, 2014, 2017; a 2025 bill reported). Check punjablaws.gov.pk for the latest text.
  • Current-affairs answers (office-holders, amendments, events of 2025–26) change quickly: verify on a news source the day before.
